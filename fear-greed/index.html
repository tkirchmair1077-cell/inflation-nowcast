4 · Method, in brief

Frozen on 22 Nov 2026, before the first evaluation

The composite is the equal-weighted mean of the component scores available on a given session, scaled 0–100. Missing inputs are omitted, not filled forward. The composite is computed only when at least three components are present. This is a staff construction that uses the usual Extreme Fear / Fear / Neutral / Greed / Extreme Greed labels (0–25, 25–45, 45–55, 55–75, 75–100). It is not the CNN Fear & Greed Index and is not affiliated with CNN.

VIX and the distance to the 50-day average are mapped with a 252-session mid-rank percentile (VIX inverted). Monthly RSI is used as is. Risk appetite is the 20-session return gap between IWM and SPY, also as a 252-session percentile. The high-yield component, when present, is an inverted z-score (756-session window) of a licensed credit spread; it is included in the composite but is not published here. Thresholds, sample split and trading rules were written down before any backtest was run and were not recalibrated afterwards.

5 · What the backtests show

Holdout from 1 Jan 2019 · backtests run 22 Nov 2026, data to 20 Nov 2026

No statistically significant edge for the predeclared trading rules: buy when the composite leaves Extreme Fear and measure the next 21 sessions; sell when it leaves Extreme Greed and measure the next 63 sessions. Signals use the close on day t, with a fictitious fill on the next close. The S&P 500 price index is used (no dividends). In-sample ends 31 Dec 2018; the holdout starts 1 Jan 2019 and was not used to set thresholds. Backtests were run on 22 Nov 2026, on data through 20 Nov 2026.

Holdout, leave-Extreme-Fear buy / 1 month: 10 signals (9 Apr 2020 to 17 Mar 2026), hit ratio 70.0% against a 68.3% base rate (bootstrap p = 0.657); mean price return +3.0% against +1.3% (p vs base = 0.133). Holdout, leave-Extreme-Greed sell / 3 months: 22 signals (25 Feb 2019 to 29 May 2026). The sell hit ratio is the share of windows with a negative price return: 27.3% against 24.0% (p = 0.734). The mean 63-session return after those signals was still +2.8%, versus +3.7% unconditionally (p = 0.279).

A second, also predeclared, test treats an entry into Extreme Fear (composite crossing below 25) as a portfolio filter on 3-month and 6-month horizons, on the S&P 500 price index and on an investment-grade credit excess-return approximation (licensed source; raw series not shown). None of these four official-threshold tests survives Holm–Bonferroni correction in the predeclared primary family (adjusted p = 1.00, 1.00, 0.819 and 1.00). An approximate White reality check on that family has p = 1.00. Inner thresholds at 20 and 15 produced some formal Holm survivors, but each had fewer than 10 holdout signals and is marked not interpretable. Use the composite as a portfolio filter, not as a trading signal.

  • Returns. Backtests use the S&P 500 price index, so long returns omit dividends (typically about 0.1–0.2 percentage points per month, or roughly 0.6–2.0 points over the 3- and 6-month horizons). The credit test is an approximation with a fixed duration of 7, not an official index excess return; convexity, defaults and financing costs are not modelled. Results are gross of transaction costs.
  • Comparisons. Previous-close, 1-week, 1-month and 1-year readings are the official composites for those sessions. When the mix of components differs from the as-of date, that is noted. Alternative same-basket values are not published, because they could be used to recover a licensed component score.
  • Inference. Forward windows overlap, so raw n overstates independent cases; n effective is also shown. Holdout samples are small (10 buy signals; 11 Extreme Fear entries). A price-return backtest is not a trading recommendation.
  • Quality. Research construction. Not investment advice, not a trading signal, and not an official index.
