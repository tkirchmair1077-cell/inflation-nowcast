Release-day note, 13 Nov 2026: October PPI (14:30 CET)

Our PPI nowcast is +0.45% m/m seasonally adjusted for final-demand PPI and +0.30% for core (final demand less foods and energy) — sealed 11 Nov 2026 09:44 Zurich, stage B1, Auditor rating YELLOW (12 Nov), not signed off / no B2 (d) — against public consensus of +0.4% m/m and +0.3%, compiled by Dow Jones as of 11 Nov 2026, and 6.6% for the 12-month headline rate (as reported by Stock Titan, published 11 Nov, 11:03 a.m. ET, consensus as of 11 Nov; the 0.4% headline also appears on Investing.com and FX Blue, and 0.4% / 0.3% on TradingCalendar (compiler not stated), checked 13 Nov 2026, 07:17–07:20 Europe/Zurich), after September’s +1.3% / +0.7% and 6.3% y/y (BLS first print, V, snapshot 11 Nov). The 2026 core PCE Q4/Q4 baseline remains 3.2% (staff judgment, retained 6 Nov and reviewed 10–12 Nov; October–December core PCE not yet published, no mechanical monthly path), with upside risk of about 3.3–3.4% (own estimate), and this release is the decision point for a reset.

Our nowcast is shown at two decimals (+0.45 / +0.30) against a one-decimal consensus (+0.4 / +0.3). The unrounded final-demand nowcast is 0.4456, which rounds to 0.4 at the consensus precision; core 0.3050 rounds to 0.3. At that precision we do not claim a difference on either measure. We also differ in the implication: a print near 0.4% / 0.3% would still leave the upside risk to the 3.2% core PCE baseline in place, given October core CPI of +0.3% m/m (BLS, 10 Nov, V), because the PPI items that matter for core PCE — PPI industry series for air passenger transportation, selected health services and other services inputs (not the final-demand aggregates) — are only in the release tables and cannot be known before 14:30.

Upside risk is a firm reading in those services components, consistent with import prices ex-fuels at 6.0% y/y in September (d, FRED vintage 16 Oct) and firmer Richmond survey prices (staff leading-indicators read, 13 Nov, 07:05 Europe/Zurich). Downside risk is a payback in final-demand energy after September’s +10.8% (BLS, V), which could pull the headline below consensus without easing the core PCE pipeline.
